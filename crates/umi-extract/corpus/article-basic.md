# How the sea keeps time

Tides are the slowest clock most people ever watch, and they are also the most accurate one available before the invention of the pendulum. A harbour master in 1740 could tell you the hour from the water line on a wall.

## Reading the wall

The marks are cut at every foot. Two of them are cut deeper, one for the spring tide and one for the neap, and the deeper cuts were maintained by the same family for four generations.

- Spring tides follow the new and full moon.
- Neap tides follow the quarters.
- The lag between the moon and the water is called the *establishment*.

> The sea does not hurry and it is never late.

A modern gauge records the same thing to the millimetre and nobody reads it, which is a fair summary of what measurement has done to attention. See the [gauge notes](https://corpus.example/tides/gauge) or the [longer history](https://example.org/history) for more.
